# Section playbooks

Per-section execution, keyed by register. `page-architecture.md` already made the case for the
order and what each section depends on; this file starts from that decision and asks a narrower
question eleven times: given this section's job, what does actually writing it look like in each of
the five registers in `../../audience-research/references/register-profiles.md`?

Read the brief's Register line once, then for every section below read only that one row. The other
four rows are what a page in a different buying situation would need — not alternatives to weigh,
and not five tones on one construction. Where a row says a section is built differently rather than
voiced differently, that's the point: the framework decided what the section proves, and the
register decided what artifact proves it, and those are two different decisions.

Each section starts with **Must accomplish** and **Must not do** — the framework-level verdict,
true regardless of register — and then the five-register table.

## Hero

**Must accomplish:** state discomfort, vision and path as one compressed claim specific enough to
survive the "now you can…" test (mentally prefix the line — if what follows names a concrete new
ability the reader didn't have before, it passes; if it only redescribes a feature, rewrite it), and
match whatever the traffic source already promised this specific visitor.

**Must not do:** promise a vision bigger than Problem and Outcome can actually earn back further
down the page, or let a second, equally-weighted call to action compete with the primary one above
the fold.

| Register | Execution |
|---|---|
| Conservative B2B (DACH) | The compressed promise stays understated — credentials-adjacent, not excited. Formal address from the first line; a single informal slip here costs more than the rest of the page reads well, per that register's Formality failure mode. |
| Technical / industrial | Leads with a specification-shaped claim — a figure, a standard, a named use case — rather than an emotional beat. The practitioner is scanning for relevance to his job before he's scanning for how anything feels. |
| Consumer / lifestyle | The vision beat is sensory and present-tense, placing the reader inside the moment. Rhythm is legitimately doing argumentative work here, not just decoration. |
| Mission / nonprofit | Names the discomfort without describing the condition the organisation exists to relieve as a lever — no implied urgency, no rescue framing this early or anywhere else. |
| Direct-response | The full compressed promise stated as arithmetic where possible, short declaratives, fragments permitted. The loudest of the five heroes, and the only register where that volume is earned rather than a mismatch. |

## Problem

**Must accomplish:** state the discomfort in the ICP's own words from the brief's Voice of customer
section, specific enough that it would not fit a competitor's page unchanged.

**Must not do:** argue the reader out of their own account of their situation before showing you
recognize it — meet the frame the reader already holds rather than correcting it first — or stay
abstract enough that Outcome has nothing concrete to contrast against.

| Register | Execution |
|---|---|
| Conservative B2B (DACH) | States the problem as a business fact with a named consequence, not a feeling — even where the feeling is real and true. |
| Technical / industrial | States the problem as a constraint or failure mode inside the reader's own process, in the field's own terms. |
| Consumer / lifestyle | Names the frustration as a specific, personal, present-tense moment — second person, concrete. |
| Mission / nonprofit | Names the condition without naming the person by it — person-first, no pity framing, no case-not-person language. |
| Direct-response | States the problem bluntly in short lines, no hedging verbs — diagnosis stated before any prescription follows. |

This is the section that moves least across registers: a real frustration in the audience's own
words is close to register-neutral in *content*. What shifts is formality and polish, which is an
editing-pass difference, not an argument one — see `page-architecture.md`'s Problem entry for why.

## Outcome

**Must accomplish:** state a concrete, measurable-or-timeframed future state that matches Hero's
promise exactly, pitched one level above whatever the reader literally asked for — the outcome they
actually want, not the literal request on the intake form.

**Must not do:** state a soft, unfalsifiable claim ("save time," "grow your business") that resists
being wrong, or quietly shift the promise from what Hero already stated.

| Register | Execution |
|---|---|
| Conservative B2B (DACH) | States the outcome with its bounding condition attached — "in a market like this, within X weeks" — never a flat promise with no stated scope. |
| Technical / industrial | States the outcome as a spec-shaped result with the operating condition it holds under; an unqualified figure is read as the worst case and disbelieved. |
| Consumer / lifestyle | Shows one specific, picturable moment rather than a superlative — "you are in the water by nine" over "an unforgettable experience." |
| Mission / nonprofit | States the outcome at the scale the organisation actually delivers it, with attribution named — what the organisation contributed, not what happened around it. |
| Direct-response | States the outcome as a number attached to a period, spoken as arithmetic rather than adjective. |

## Solution

**Must accomplish:** name what this is clearly enough that a reader could say what they'd be buying —
product, intended buyer, and what clicking does — without yet explaining why it works.

**Must not do:** explain the mechanism here (that's the next section's job, not this one), or bury
the category behind a name so clever the reader can't tell what it is.

| Register | Execution |
|---|---|
| Conservative B2B (DACH) | Names the category plainly and formally. Specificity beats cleverness; a name that needs decoding costs trust before the offer is even understood. |
| Technical / industrial | Names it with the field's own term of art, even where that term is dense — the correct word is a competence signal, not a barrier, in this register. |
| Consumer / lifestyle | Names it in inviting, benefit-adjacent language rather than a category label. |
| Mission / nonprofit | Names the programme or service plainly, participant-first, without a commercial-sounding product name. |
| Direct-response | Names it as an ownable, methodology-named offer — naming the process raises perceived likelihood before Mechanism has explained anything. |

## Mechanism

**Must accomplish:** explain *how* the outcome happens, specific to this product, answering the
doubt the reader is actually carrying — not that this works in general, but that it works for
someone in their situation. Name the method specifically enough to be repeated back; a named process
raises believability even when the substance underneath a generic one would be identical.

**Must not do:** re-assert the outcome instead of defending it, or explain the mechanism in
vocabulary that reads as jargon rather than as a competence signal in the register you're writing
for — the Mechanism section is where an unearned technical term costs the most.

| Register | Execution |
|---|---|
| Conservative B2B (DACH) | Explains the mechanism as a documented, named process with credentials behind it — proof-adjacent, not colorful. |
| Technical / industrial | Full specification depth — tables, tolerances and diagrams outrank prose wherever the content is genuinely tabular. |
| Consumer / lifestyle | One clear sentence of *why it works*, not a technical breakdown — over-explaining here flattens the feeling the page is selling. |
| Mission / nonprofit | Shows how the intervention actually reaches the person it's for, traceable end to end from input to result. |
| Direct-response | Named, numbered, walkthrough-style — the strongest proof this register has is showing exactly how the outcome gets produced, because it's the one form a competitor can't cheaply fabricate. |

## Value stack

**Must accomplish:** itemize what's actually inside the offer so price gets judged against something
concrete, and anchor legitimately — against a comparable alternative's cost, the cost of the status
quo, or what the pieces would cost separately. Each bonus, where bonuses exist, should close one
specific objection from the brief rather than pad the list.

**Must not do:** inflate a stated value past what's defensible, or force a "stack" framing onto a
register whose buyer does not evaluate offers that way.

| Register | Execution |
|---|---|
| Conservative B2B (DACH) | A scope table — included, excluded, priced or scoped per line. Anchors against a stated cost of inaction, never a crossed-out "total value" figure, which reads as a device in this register. |
| Technical / industrial | A specification or compatibility matrix. The "stack" here is the spec sheet itself, not a value narrative laid over it. |
| Consumer / lifestyle | "What's included," shown with specifics and photography, stacked by benefit rather than by dollar figure. |
| Mission / nonprofit | No stack framing at all. Tiered participation with a concrete stated allocation per level ("$50 provides X") — a value comparison here reads as commercial pressure on a cause. |
| Direct-response | The full theatrical stack — itemized components, stated values, total-value-then-price reveal, anchored against a named alternative. |

This is one of the three sections — with Guarantee and CTA — where the register difference is a
different construction, not a different tone on one construction. See `page-architecture.md`'s Value
stack entry for why a mismatch here is a wrong-argument failure rather than an editing one.

## Proof

**Must accomplish:** corroborate the Mechanism and Value stack claims with evidence from outside the
seller's own copy, positioned next to the specific claim it backs rather than swept into one block.

**Must not do:** run an unattributed testimonial (missing name, role or company), or present a
best-case result as though it were typical without saying so.

| Register | Execution |
|---|---|
| Conservative B2B (DACH) | A named person with a checkable role and employer; a document the reader can inspect without contacting anyone. Proof by adjective doesn't count. |
| Technical / industrial | Datasheets, cited test-standard designations, tolerance ranges. A testimonial standing in this slot reads as a substitution for missing data, not as weaker proof. |
| Consumer / lifestyle | Real photography, reviews with visible volume, recency and platform — not a curated wall of five-star quotes, which reads as filtered. |
| Mission / nonprofit | Both an individual, dignity-consented account and a figure with a denominator — one alone reads as either deflection or as compliance with no human referent. |
| Direct-response | Numbers with a mechanism attached — dated screenshots, denominators shown next to percentages, a stated method the reader could rerun. |

This section carries the most detailed register treatment in the whole plugin because
`register-profiles.md` gives Proof its own fully worked dimension per archetype — read that
dimension in full before writing this section; the table above is a pointer into it, not a
replacement for it.

## Objections

**Must accomplish:** run the brief's ranked Objections list through the chain in
`objection-to-belief.md` — surface objection, hidden objection, belief shift, proof — and resolve
the load-bearing ones on the page itself, not only in the FAQ.

**Must not do:** answer the surface objection while leaving the hidden one untouched (the classic
miss: answering "how much does it cost" while the real objection was "will my team actually use
this"), or manufacture an objection the reader never carried just to have something to resolve.

| Register | Execution |
|---|---|
| Conservative B2B (DACH) | Lets the proof already on the page imply the shift. Never narrates "you might be thinking X" — the narration itself reads as a technique being run on the reader in this register. |
| Technical / industrial | Resolves via a specification or compatibility answer, not reassurance language — "it connects to your ERP version" beats "don't worry, integration is easy." |
| Consumer / lifestyle | Can name the doubt directly and answer it in the same breath, conversationally. |
| Mission / nonprofit | Addresses only what a genuine participant or funder would actually ask, in a tone that is never defensive. |
| Direct-response | States the objection out loud, reframes it bluntly, attaches proof immediately after — the full chain visible on the page in that order. |

## Guarantee

**Must accomplish:** reverse a stated, concrete risk with terms a reader could actually hold the
business to, sequenced after Proof and Objections so it reinforces a case already made rather than
compensating for one that wasn't.

**Must not do:** offer a generic "satisfaction guaranteed" with no conditions, or promise terms the
business could not sustain if a real fraction of buyers invoked them.

| Register | Execution |
|---|---|
| Conservative B2B (DACH) | States commitment mechanics plainly instead — what happens after submitting, when, and the exact point of commitment. Per that register's CTA-directness dimension, an ambiguous commitment point costs more here than a modest guarantee would. |
| Technical / industrial | An SLA-shaped guarantee tied to a measurable term where the category supports one, or none at all where it doesn't — a vague guarantee is worse than none in this register. |
| Consumer / lifestyle | A plain, unconditional refund window, stated simply, no legalese. |
| Mission / nonprofit | Not a commercial "guarantee" — a transparency commitment (how funds are used, what gets reported) does the equivalent trust-building work without pricing the cause. |
| Direct-response | Explicit, structural risk reversal with its conditions and duration stated — part of the offer's architecture, never a footnote. |

## CTA

**Must accomplish:** ask for one action, worded as the outcome of clicking rather than the mechanics
of the click, repeated at natural stopping points — matched to how this buyer actually decides
(alone in one sitting, brought to someone else first, or arriving at different depths of interest).

**Must not do:** run several competing, equally-weighted primary actions, or label the button with
the mechanics of the click ("Submit," "Click Here") in the slot where the outcome belongs.

| Register | Execution |
|---|---|
| Conservative B2B (DACH) | One primary action, in the same words every time, with a lower-commitment second option beside it — most buyers here have to bring this to someone else first. |
| Technical / industrial | Multiple entry points at different depths, each explicitly labeled which is which — datasheet, sample, or engineer call. Gate the application support, never the specification itself. |
| Consumer / lifestyle | One obvious, low-friction action, with the price visible before the click. |
| Mission / nonprofit | Several parallel, non-competing participation paths on one page — donate, volunteer, refer, request support — never one donate button asked of every reader regardless of why they came. |
| Direct-response | One commanded action, with its explicit risk reversal stated right beside it. |

## FAQ

**Must accomplish:** answer real, lower-priority objections that Objections didn't resolve, plus the
category-standard questions a search or AI-answer surface would expect this kind of page to cover.

**Must not do:** pad the section with unrelated trivia that does no persuasive work, or answer a
question in a way that contradicts what the Objections section already resolved.

| Register | Execution |
|---|---|
| Conservative B2B (DACH) | Formal question phrasing; answers cite the same checkable artifacts Proof already used. |
| Technical / industrial | Questions phrased the way a practitioner actually asks them; answers as specification-precise as the Mechanism section. |
| Consumer / lifestyle | Conversational question phrasing, short answers. |
| Mission / nonprofit | Questions a genuine participant or funder would actually raise; answers held to the same dignity standard as the rest of the page. |
| Direct-response | Blunt question phrasing, short numbers-forward answers. |

Mark the section up as `FAQPage` schema where the platform supports it. That's a technical-SEO
decision, not a register one — every archetype benefits from it equally, and `conversion-page-audit`
checks for it independently of anything in this file.
