# Hook taxonomy

Eight types. Each names the **claim a line makes** — the angle, the thing being asserted about the
reader or the offer. Under each type, what it promises, when it earns its place, when it turns on
you, and the same claim taken through all five registers.

## The boundary: this file decides the claim

**This file decides the claim. `headline-formulas.md` decides the sentence.**

Whether this reader can be told this thing at all is settled here, once, per type per register. What
shape the telling takes, and whether the surface it has to fit will hold that shape, is settled
there. The two meet at one point and only one: a type chosen here becomes a construction chosen
there. When a construction turns out to have no slot for what a register requires the line to carry,
that is a fact about the sentence rather than about the reader, and the verdict on it belongs to
`headline-formulas.md` — it is not repeated here.

Which of these types belongs in a hero, a subject line or a button is also not this file's business.
Surfaces bind before register does, and `headline-formulas.md` opens with them.

## The standing cast

Five illustrative businesses, one per register, used throughout so the contrast down a register
column is legible across all eight types. They are invented for this file; the figures in them are
placeholders showing where a real figure would sit, never claims.

| Register | Standing example |
| --- | --- |
| `Conservative B2B (DACH)` | A German firm preparing medical-device manufacturers for regulatory audits |
| `Technical / industrial` | A maker of inline weld-seam inspection cameras |
| `Consumer / lifestyle` | A two-day guided sea-kayaking trip |
| `Mission / nonprofit` | A regional food bank that also runs a benefits-advice service |
| `Direct-response` | A self-serve course teaching freelance designers to sell retainers |

The register is not a coat of paint on one business. A product sold into a formal-address committee
purchase and a product sold to one person deciding alone in a single session are different products,
and pretending otherwise is what produces five translations of one line. Read down a column to see
what a register does; read across a row to see what a type does.

**Where a register removes a type's defining move**, the row says so and shows what stands in its
place, marked **`SUBSTITUTED`**. That is a finding about the register, not a gap in the file, and it
is the most useful thing here — a substituted row tells you the type is the wrong instrument before
you have spent an afternoon polishing it.

### 1. Outcome

**Promises:** the result itself, with nothing else in the sentence.

**Earns its place** when the result is the thing in shortest supply in the reader's head — he knows
the category, he has seen the competitors, and what he cannot picture is the finished state.

**Turns on you** when the result is one every competitor also claims, at which point the line has
described the category rather than the offer, and the reader has no reason to keep reading.

- **`Conservative B2B (DACH)`** — the result is stated at or slightly under what can be evidenced,
  and it is a result the reader could confirm without contacting anyone.
  > *Your next audit closes without a documentation finding.*
- **`Technical / industrial`** — the result is a measured parameter with the operating window
  printed beside it, because a bare figure and a bounded one are not the same claim in this register.
  > *Seam defects down to 0.3 mm, caught at 1.2 m/s line speed, on stainless and coated stock.*
- **`Consumer / lifestyle`** — the result is a scene the reader is standing inside. A number would
  flatten it rather than sharpen it.
  > *Day two, you paddle out before the wind gets up and eat lunch on a beach nobody can drive to.*
- **`Mission / nonprofit`** — the result is attributed and carries a denominator. It reports this
  organisation's own share of the change, not the change.
  > *1,340 households collected food here last year. 611 left with a benefits appointment booked.*
- **`Direct-response`** — the result is stated as a sum the reader can do in his head, interval included.
  > *Two retainers at £1,500 a month, signed inside eight weeks.*

**The contrast worth looking at:** `Conservative B2B (DACH)` drops the number and keeps the fact,
`Consumer / lifestyle` drops the number and keeps the image, `Technical / industrial` keeps the
number and adds what makes it checkable, and `Direct-response` *is* the number.

### 2. Time

**Promises:** an interval — how long until the result, or how long the reader is committed for.

**Earns its place** when the reader's real objection is duration rather than doubt: he believes it
works and does not believe it works soon enough to matter this quarter.

**Turns on you** when the interval is a hope rather than a policy. A time claim is the easiest
promise to make and the one a client is most often held to in writing.

- **`Conservative B2B (DACH)`** — **`SUBSTITUTED`.** A speed promise about *his* result is both a
  commitment he will hold you to and a device he distrusts. What survives is an interval in your own
  process, stated as a fact about how the work runs.
  > *You have the gap analysis in writing within five working days of handing over the file.*
- **`Technical / industrial`** — time is procurement information and publishing it is the whole
  point; the quiet loss in this register is the specification that goes to whoever printed their
  lead time.
  > *Six weeks from drawing to a commissioned line, qualification run included.*
- **`Consumer / lifestyle`** — the clock is the shape of the reader's day rather than a measurement
  of anything.
  > *On the water at eight, first beach by half past ten, and no schedule after that.*
- **`Mission / nonprofit`** — **`SUBSTITUTED`.** How fast someone's circumstances improve is not the
  organisation's claim to make. The usable interval is the organisation's own responsiveness, or a
  deadline that genuinely closes.
  > *Come in on a Tuesday and someone sits down with you on the Thursday.*
- **`Direct-response`** — the interval is half the claim and is stated as a bound, not an average.
  > *Your first retainer proposal goes out on day four. Signed inside thirty.*

**The contrast worth looking at:** in three registers the clock is on the seller — a process
interval, a lead time, a response time. In `Consumer / lifestyle` it is on the reader's day. Only in
`Direct-response` is it on the reader's result, which is exactly why that is the register where a
missed deadline becomes public.

### 3. Effort

**Promises:** what the reader does *not* have to do.

**Earns its place** when the obstacle is work rather than money or doubt — the reader believes the
outcome and cannot see where the hours come from.

**Turns on you** when the removed effort is one the reader was not dreading, which reads as a
stranger guessing at his week.

- **`Conservative B2B (DACH)`** — a *without* aimed at his existing process is a verdict on a choice
  he made and may have to defend internally. Point it at added work instead, never at what he
  already runs.
  > *No change to your existing document structure. We work inside it.*
- **`Technical / industrial`** — effort is integration cost: retooling, downtime, requalification.
  This is among the strongest lines available in the register, because it is the cost that kills
  otherwise-suitable selections.
  > *Retrofits to the existing frame. No requalification of the line.*
- **`Consumer / lifestyle`** — effort is faff, and naming the exact faff is what lands. Generic ease
  is invisible.
  > *We carry the tents. You carry a dry bag and lunch.*
- **`Mission / nonprofit`** — the removed effort is a barrier to access, and the line is addressed to
  the person who needs the service rather than to the donor reading over their shoulder.
  > *No referral, no appointment, no proof of income. Tuesday and Friday, the door is open.*
- **`Direct-response`** — the removed effort is the objection that would otherwise stop the click,
  named the way the reader names it to himself.
  > *Land retainers without a portfolio rebuild, a niche, or one cold email.*

**The contrast worth looking at:** the register decides *whose* effort is being removed — his team's,
the plant's, the traveller's, the applicant's, the reader's own. Get the owner wrong and the line is
addressed to somebody who is not reading.

### 4. Callout

**Promises:** that the line is for a particular person, and by naming them, not for everyone else.

**Earns its place** when traffic is mixed and the cost of the wrong reader is high — an expensive
consultation, a limited cohort, a service with eligibility.

**Turns on you** when the named group is so broad that naming it excludes nobody, which spends the
strongest position on the page to say *hello*.

- **`Conservative B2B (DACH)`** — the callout is a scope statement rather than an invitation. It
  names role and situation and then stops; *this is for you* is a register tell from somewhere else.
  > *For quality managers at Class IIa manufacturers preparing a first audit under the new regime.*
- **`Technical / industrial`** — the callout names the application, because application is how this
  reader decides in one second whether the page concerns him.
  > *For longitudinal seam welding on thin-wall tube, 0.8 to 3 mm.*
- **`Consumer / lifestyle`** — the callout names a self-image the reader would happily claim, and it
  is allowed to be funny about it.
  > *For people who can swim, have never kayaked, and would rather not be taught in a leisure centre.*
- **`Mission / nonprofit`** — **`SUBSTITUTED`.** A callout that names people by their condition is
  the register's signature failure, visible instantly to the community and invisible to whoever
  wrote it. What is available is a callout by *relationship to the organisation* — and this register
  needs several of them visible at once rather than one.
  > *If this week is the problem rather than this month, this page is for you.*
  > *If you run a payroll and want to fund a delivery round, this one is.*
- **`Direct-response`** — the callout is the sharpest of the five and carries its own disqualifier,
  because excluding people is what makes the included ones lean in.
  > *Freelance designers billing £3k to £8k a month on projects. Not agencies. Not juniors.*

**The contrast worth looking at:** `Conservative B2B (DACH)` calls out a *situation*,
`Direct-response` calls out a *number*, `Technical / industrial` calls out an *application*, and
`Mission / nonprofit` calls out a *relationship* and may never call out a condition. Four different
things wearing one type's name.

### 5. Contrarian

**Promises:** that something the reader believes, or has been told, is wrong.

**Earns its place** when the market has a genuine consensus that is costing the reader money, and
you can show it. The value is the correction, not the disagreement.

**Turns on you** when the contradicted belief is one the reader holds personally and has acted on,
because the next thing he does is defend it — and he defends it by leaving.

- **`Conservative B2B (DACH)`** — the target is a practice in the market, never the reader's own
  judgement, and it arrives as a finding with its evidence attached rather than as an opinion.
  > *Most audit findings we see are not documentation errors. They are change-control records nobody
  > closed.*
- **`Technical / industrial`** — contrarian works when it corrects how a published figure is read: a
  spec measured under conditions no real installation reproduces.
  > *Rated detection accuracy is measured on stationary parts. Yours are moving.*
- **`Consumer / lifestyle`** — contrarian is light, and it is usually about the category rather than
  about the reader.
  > *The best day is the second one. Which is why we do not sell a day trip.*
- **`Mission / nonprofit`** — the belief challenged belongs to the funding or policy environment and
  the line is addressed to funders. Never a belief about the people served, and never phrased so that
  someone who uses the service finds themselves inside the argument.
  > *Food is the cheapest part of what this costs us. The advice appointment is what stops the next
  > visit.*
- **`Direct-response`** — native, and aimed squarely at the reader's own behaviour, which is the one
  register where that is expected rather than insulting.
  > *Raising your day rate is not the fix. You are selling the wrong unit.*

**The contrast worth looking at:** the register decides *who is allowed to be wrong*. In
`Direct-response` it is the reader. In `Conservative B2B (DACH)` and `Technical / industrial` it is
the market and its measurement conventions. In `Mission / nonprofit` it is the funder's model, and
never the person served.

### 6. Pain

**Promises:** that you can describe the reader's discomfort more accurately than he expected.

**Earns its place** when the reader has not yet named the problem himself, and recognition is the
thing that makes him keep reading.

**Turns on you** when it is written from the outside, in words the seller reaches for and the
reader never would, at which point it reads as a stranger explaining his own week to him.

- **`Conservative B2B (DACH)`** — the pain must be one he would state himself in a meeting, in
  process terms. Personal-failure framing is unusable: his credibility is precisely what he is
  protecting by buying anything at all.
  > *The questions arrive in week one, and the file has not been opened since the last release.*
- **`Technical / industrial`** — the pain is a failure mode with a mechanism, not a feeling.
  > *The defect that gets through is the one that only shows up after the coating goes on.*
- **`Consumer / lifestyle`** — pain is friction and it is held light. Heavy pain in a discretionary
  purchase gives the reader a reason to stop thinking about the purchase at all.
  > *The trouble with a day trip is that you spend most of it getting there.*
- **`Mission / nonprofit`** — **`SUBSTITUTED`, and the substitution changes the reader.** Naming a
  beneficiary's pain back to them is describing rather than addressing them; naming it to a donor turns
  hardship into a fundraising instrument. No beneficiary-facing pain hook is offered here. What remains is the funder's own pain, which is a different person reading.
  > *You fund the parcels and never find out whether the same households came back.*
- **`Direct-response`** — the pain is stated in the reader's own words and is allowed to sting,
  because this reader is fluent in the register and reads it as accuracy rather than pressure.
  > *You finish a project and the pipeline is empty again. Every time.*

**The contrast worth looking at:** this is the only type where a register removes the move outright
rather than toning it down, and the substitute swaps the reader as well as the line. If you find
yourself writing a beneficiary-facing pain hook, the type is wrong, not the wording.

### 7. Mechanism

**Promises:** that there is a named method behind the result, and that it is repeatable.

**Earns its place** when the reader believes the outcome is possible and doubts it is possible
*reliably* — the difference between a good result and a good process.

**Turns on you** when the mechanism is invented for the page. A named method nobody actually follows
is a promise the delivery team has to keep.

- **`Conservative B2B (DACH)`** — the strongest type available in this register, because a
  describable method is what he forwards to the person who has to approve it. The mechanism does his
  internal argument for him.
  > *Three passes, in order: change control, design history, labelling. Findings cascade in that
  > direction, so the work runs in it too.*
- **`Technical / industrial`** — the mechanism is the physical principle, and naming it correctly is
  a competence test that runs before any persuasion does.
  > *Structured light at 4 kHz, so the profile is captured between vibration cycles instead of
  > averaged across them.*
- **`Consumer / lifestyle`** — **`SUBSTITUTED`.** Explaining the machinery drains the mood the page
  was built to produce, and converts a decision the reader wanted to take quickly into an appraisal.
  Mechanism survives only where the method is itself part of the experience, at which point the line
  is closer to proof than to mechanism: it names *who is doing it*, not *how the system works*.
  > *Two guides, six people, and one of the guides grew up on this coast.*
- **`Mission / nonprofit`** — mechanism is the programme model, and it is the section an institutional
  funder actually reads before the impact figures.
  > *Every parcel is handed over by someone trained to ask one question about benefits, and the
  > appointment is booked in that same conversation.*
- **`Direct-response`** — mechanism is the named system, and its job is to make the result look
  repeatable rather than lucky.
  > *The retainer conversion is one email, sent at one point in the project. That is the method.*

**The contrast worth looking at:** mechanism strength tracks whether the decision has to be defended
to a third party. `Conservative B2B (DACH)` and `Mission / nonprofit` want it most, because a
committee and a funder respectively will ask. `Consumer / lifestyle` wants it least, because nobody
is going to ask the reader to justify a weekend.

### 8. Transformation

**Promises:** a before and an after, in one sentence.

**Earns its place** when the gap between the two states is the whole value, and when both ends are
things the reader can recognise.

**Turns on you** when the before state is one the reader would be embarrassed to be seen in, which
converts a hook into an accusation.

- **`Conservative B2B (DACH)`** — both states are facts about the organisation that a third party
  could check. A before-and-after about a *person* is out of register regardless of how true it is.
  > *Fourteen open corrective actions, nine with no owner. One quarter later: three open, all dated.*
- **`Technical / industrial`** — **`SUBSTITUTED`.** This reader does not think in narrative
  before-and-after; he thinks in a delta on one measured parameter under held conditions. The type
  converts to a paired measurement, and the held conditions are what make it a claim rather than an
  anecdote.
  > *Escape rate on the coated line: 1.9% before, 0.2% after. Same shift pattern, same operators.*
- **`Consumer / lifestyle`** — transformation is a mood shift across the trip, present tense,
  sensory, and small enough to be believable.
  > *The first morning you keep checking your phone. By the second you have stopped drying your hands
  > first.*
- **`Mission / nonprofit`** — **`SUBSTITUTED`.** The beneficiary before-and-after is the most
  damaging line available in this register: it moves a certain kind of donor and forfeits the confidence of
  every other party the organisation depends on. It is usable only when the person tells it
  themselves and has evidently agreed to it. Otherwise the subject of the transformation is the
  system, not a person.
  > *Two years ago this was a parcel and a leaflet. Now two in three people who take a parcel leave
  > with an appointment.*
- **`Direct-response`** — the reader's own before-and-after, numbers on both ends, and it is the
  register's most natural sentence.
  > *From chasing three proposals a month to two clients on retainer and one call a week.*

**The contrast worth looking at:** two registers change what the transformation is *about* rather
than how it sounds — `Technical / industrial` swaps the narrative for a measurement pair, and
`Mission / nonprofit` swaps the person for the system. The other three keep the shape.

## Index: where each type sits, per register

Derived from the rows above; it is a lookup, not a second argument. Read the type's row before
trusting a cell.

| Register | Strongest types | Substituted or constrained |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Conservative B2B (DACH)` | Mechanism, outcome, callout | Time (process interval only), contrarian (market, not reader), transformation (organisation, not person) |
| `Technical / industrial` | Outcome-as-spec, effort, mechanism | Transformation (measurement pair, not narrative) |
| `Consumer / lifestyle` | Outcome, time, effort, transformation | Mechanism (provenance stands in), pain (kept light) |

| `Direct-response` | All eight | None — which is why this is the register that flatters a bad set of variants |

## Two types that collide

Variants are only variants if they differ in what they claim. Three pairs regularly produce two
lines that read as one:

- **Contrarian and pain** are both a negative diagnosis. Offer one of them per set, or the second
  reads as the first restated more slowly.
- **Outcome and time** collapse when the time claim carries no interval the outcome did not already
  imply. If the outcome line already says *this quarter*, the time line has nothing left to add.
- **Effort and mechanism** are the same fact from two ends — the mechanism is *why* the effort is
  removed. Running both is fine when the mechanism line names something the effort line did not, and
  is padding when it does not.

When a set of variants gets rejected as *they all sound the same*, check this list before rewriting.
The usual cause is two types from one pair, not weak prose.
