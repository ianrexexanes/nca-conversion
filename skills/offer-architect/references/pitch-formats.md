# Pitch formats

The pitch is the offer said out loud, at three lengths. This file covers the spoken and sent forms —
a line in a bio, a paragraph in an email or proposal, the full argument on a call or in a deck. Page
copy is a different job with its own section structure and belongs to
`../../conversion-page-build/SKILL.md`; do not write a landing page here.

## Which length, and why it is not a preference

Length is decided by two things, neither of them taste:

- **How much the reader already knows.** A reader who has never heard of the category needs the
  problem established before the promise means anything. A reader who arrived because they were
  looking for exactly this needs the promise and the terms and nothing else.
- **How much room the surface gives.** A bio line, an email opener, a slide, and a forty-minute call
  are four different amounts of room, and the pitch that fits one is not the other three trimmed.

## The compression rule

**The short version is not the long version summarised. The long version's load-bearing claim is the
short version, extracted.** Everything else in the long version exists to support that one claim.

Which means the order you write them in depends on what you know:

- **Offer is new, or the diagnosis was surprising: write long first.** You find out which claim is
  actually load-bearing by building the whole argument and seeing what the rest is propping up. Then
  lift it.
- **Offer is proven and the claim is already known: write short first**, then expand — because you
  are no longer discovering the claim, you are supporting it, and starting long invites padding.

A short version that is not derived from a long one is a slogan. It will be fluent, it will be
memorable, and it will not survive the first buyer who asks a question.

## Short — one or two lines

Carries exactly three things: **who this is for, what they end up with, and the one fact that makes
that believable.** The believability fact is what separates a pitch from a claim, and it is the part
that gets dropped first under length pressure. Keep it.

Must not: carry the stack, name the price, or list what is included. A short pitch that tries to
carry inclusions becomes a compressed list, and a compressed list is the least persuasive thing a
sentence can be.

## Medium — a paragraph or a section

The working length: an email, a proposal summary, a section of a deck, an answer to "so what do you
actually do."

Five beats, in this order. **The order is the argument** — each beat earns the next, and reordering
them means the reader is being asked to accept something before the reason for accepting it has
arrived.

1. The situation the reader is in, in terms they would recognise as their own.
2. What they end up with instead, concretely.
3. How it works — the mechanism, briefly. This is the beat that gets cut and should not be; it is
   doing more for belief than anything else in the paragraph.
4. What that is grounded in — one piece of proof, the strongest available per
   `../../conversion-page-build/references/proof-library.md`.
5. The ask, singular and specific.

## Long — the full argument

Everything the medium version has, plus what it did not have room to establish: the problem developed
far enough that the reader recognises the cost of leaving it alone, the alternatives they have already
tried and why those did not hold, the stack, the price with its contrast, the terms of sale, and the
ranked objections answered before the reader raises them.

Two placement rules that matter more than the rest of the sequence:

- **The stack comes before the number.** A price meeting a reader who has not yet added anything up
  is a price with nothing to be compared against, and the reader compares it to the first thing that
  comes to mind, which is usually a competitor.
- **The number is not the last thing before the ask.** Put the terms of sale after it — what happens
  if it does not work, what the buyer's exit is. Ending on the price leaves the reader's last thought
  on what it costs them, and in the conservative registers that is the thought that produces "let me
  think about it."

## Objections belong in the pitch, not after it

Take the top three or four from the brief's ranked Objections and answer them inside the pitch,
positioned where the reader would naturally raise them. An objection answered before it is voiced
reads as understanding; the same answer given after the reader raises it reads as a rebuttal, and
rebuttals are argued with. `../../conversion-page-build/references/objection-to-belief.md` has the
chain from surface objection to the belief that has to shift.

## By register

The five beats do not change. What changes is where the weight sits and what the ask can be. Read
only the row the brief named.

| Register | Where the weight sits | The ask |
|---|---|---|
| Conservative B2B (DACH) | Credentials and scope before promise. Understate the claim, name the person accountable, and state what is excluded as clearly as what is included. Terms of sale get real airtime rather than a footnote. | A next step with a defined agenda and a defined length. An open-ended "let's talk" costs this buyer an unbounded commitment and gets declined for that reason alone. |
| Technical / industrial | The mechanism, at length, with figures and their operating conditions. This reader will spend the time and resents having it summarised for him. | A technical conversation or an evaluation, not a sales call. Say which. |
| Consumer / lifestyle | The outcome and the picture of it. Proof is social rather than documentary; the mechanism is one line, not a section. | Direct and low-friction, stated once and repeated. |
| Mission / nonprofit | What the work accomplishes and how it is accountable. The problem beat is stated without turning the people it concerns into a lever. | Several participation levels offered together rather than one escalating ask — this register's readers are split by definition and routing them apart reads as gatekeeping. |
| Direct-response | The promise first, then the proof, then the stack, then the contrast. The strongest claim goes at the top and the argument spends the rest of its length earning it back. | Immediate, repeated, and unambiguous. |

## Before it goes out

If a voice or humanising skill is available in this environment, route the pitch through it — the
same rule `conversion-page-build` applies to page copy. A pitch is read by a person and carries the
same tells as any other written argument. If nothing is installed, apply
`../../copy-sweeps/references/banned-vocabulary.md` directly instead. One of the two always runs.
