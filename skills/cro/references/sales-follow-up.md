# Sales follow-up

Read this for the follow-up leg of `/cro diagnose`, the part that starts once the visitor has sent the form: what the first call asks, how fast the lead hears back, which next step was promised, and what the confirmation says. Claude reads it and puts the questions to the owner. It never contacts a lead, and it cannot hear the owner's calls, so what it reports from them is the owner's word.

## The discovery-call questions

A page can only bring someone to the call. What turns the call into a sale is the buyer hearing themselves explain why the problem matters. The questions come in one order, one at a time, and the seller listens to each answer before the next. The kinds, with an archetype example each:

- **How things stand now.** Keep these few. The seller does the homework first and never asks what a quick look at the website could have told them. A bookkeeping practice might ask: "How do you keep track of receipts and invoices today?"
- **What is going wrong.** The seller asks where the current way is slow, unreliable or unwelcome. The same practice: "What happens when the month ends and the receipts are still in a drawer?"
- **What it costs if nothing changes.** The seller asks the buyer to follow the problem through to its effects on time, money, risk and other people. For example: "If the books are still late next quarter, what does that do to your tax bill, and to your evenings?"
- **What solving it would be worth, said by the buyer.** The buyer states the benefit, which persuades far more than the seller doing it. For example: "If this were off your desk, what would you do with the time?"

Take care with these. Do not ask the buyer to value something the business cannot deliver, because that builds a want a rival will meet. And a call that ends in "we will be in touch" has gone nowhere, so it ends with a specific next step the buyer agrees to, which is the next section.

For the diagnosis, the leg asks the owner which of these the first call covers, in what order, and whether the answers are written down. Calls that open with a pitch, or that end with no agreed step, are findings.

## Response time

The rule: a lead hears back within minutes, not days. Interest fades quickly, and a visitor who sent the form has usually asked other businesses too.

A named person on the team replies, with a named stand-in, not a shared inbox that nobody owns. The automatic message is the confirmation, not the reply. When nobody can reply soon, because it is outside working hours or the team is away, the confirmation says so plainly, says when the reply will come, and gives another way to reach the business.

The leg cannot time a reply from outside. It asks the owner when the last enquiries arrived and when each was answered, since the timestamps are theirs to read, and it checks whether the page or the form promises a time.

## The promised next step

The page and the form promise one next step: a call, a quote, a visit. The same words appear on the button, above the form, in the confirmation and in the first reply, and the follow-up keeps that promise. Example: a dental clinic's button says "Book a check-up", the confirmation says the clinic will ring to find a time, and the first reply offers times. A gap looks like a button that says "Get a quote" followed by a reply that asks the visitor to fill in a long questionnaire. The visitor was promised one thing and handed another.

## The confirmation message

The thank-you step on the page and the confirmation email both say what happens next and when: who will be in touch, by what route, in what timeframe in plain words such as "today" or "by the end of the next working day", and what to do if nothing arrives. `/cro form` reads the thank-you step for exactly this, so this section and that reading agree.

A bare "Thanks, we got your message" fails, because it tells the visitor nothing about what follows. A passing example: "Thanks, Sam. Someone from the clinic will ring you today, before the end of the afternoon, to find a time. If you have heard nothing by then, write to hello@clinic.example."
